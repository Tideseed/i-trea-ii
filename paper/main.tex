\begin{abstract}
\pending{Abstract to be written after the confirmatory experiment.} Users of mixed-integer programming (MIP) solvers
often need a good feasible solution within a fixed time rather than a proof of optimality. We study whether an
evolutionary algorithm that runs on a GPU, coupled with an exact MIP solver so that the two exchange solutions in both
directions while they run, finds good solutions sooner than either method alone. The population keeps a capped share
of infeasible solutions whose objective is better than the best feasible one, an idea carried over from the
interactive three-rank evolutionary algorithm I-TREA~\citep{orbay2011itrea}. The hypotheses, arms, instance-pool rules,
metrics and analysis were fixed before any experiment. This draft describes the design; it contains no results.
\end{abstract}
