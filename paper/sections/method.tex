\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
This section describes the configuration fixed after phase~1 (file and parameters: Appendix~\ref{sec:repro}): three
processes on one machine, two of which share the GPU (Figure~\ref{fig:system}).

\paragraph{Processes and exchange.} SCIP~10~\citep{hojny2025scip10} runs with one thread on one CPU core. The EA runs
on a second core and the GPU, and cuOpt~26.08~\citep{cuopt} with three threads on three smaller cores and the same GPU.
All three start together once each has read the instance. SCIP sends the EA its incumbent, up to eight
pool solutions, its proven bound, and LP solutions of its tree nodes, including infeasible ones better than the
incumbent. The EA sends SCIP, through a heuristic plug-in, every solution better than SCIP's incumbent. cuOpt streams
its incumbents to the EA but receives nothing: during the solve, cuOpt~26.08 accepts outside solutions only through a
callback that switches off its presolve, which in our predecessor study made a two-way exchange with cuOpt lose 14.7\,\%~\citep{orbay2026optopt}.

\paragraph{Population and ranks.} The EA keeps up to 256 individuals in three ranks. \emph{Rank~1} protects the best
10\,\% of the feasible solutions. \emph{Rank~0} holds up to 10\,\% infeasible solutions whose objective is better than
the incumbent, the best of the EA's, SCIP's and cuOpt's. A Rank~0 member whose objective is better than SCIP's proven
bound cannot become feasible at that value, so it is removed. \emph{Rank~2} fills the rest from the new candidates,
ranked by objective and constraint violation, and tournaments prefer Rank~1, then Rank~0, then Rank~2.

\paragraph{Variation and repair.} Children come from crossover, which mostly keeps the values both parents agree on,
and from mutation of about four integer variables. A feasibility-jump local search~\citep{luteberget2023feasibility}
then repairs each child on the GPU, one thread block per child. It moves one variable at a time to reduce a weighted
row violation; once an incumbent exists, an objective row keeps the repair from giving up objective value. A
\emph{repair clock} picks each generation's repair depth from the objective gain per second measured at each depth.

\paragraph{LP solutions and the last two changes of phase~1.} A HiGHS process~\citep{huangfu2018parallelizing,highs}
on the EA's core solves the LP relaxation once, and repaired roundings of its solution join the population. Two
changes make better use of that single core:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{LP decoding.} A second HiGHS process fixes the integer values of up to four candidates per generation and
  re-optimises their continuous variables, once the LP relaxation has finished.
\item \textbf{Yielding wait.} By default the EA's host thread spins while it waits for the GPU. With CUDA's yield flag
  it gives its core to any other runnable process; in a check, a competing process got the whole core instead of
  about half.
\end{itemize}
