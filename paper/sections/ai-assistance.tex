\section{AI assistance statement}\label{sec:ai}
\paragraph{Who did what.} The author set the question and the direction and took the decisions that changed the
study, among them the go at checkpoint C1 (D-033). AgentRA (Agent Research Assistant: Claude Code with Anthropic's
Claude Opus~5.5) did the rest under his direction: design and pre-registration, the \texttt{itrea} package and tests,
job files, every timed run, report tools and analyses, decision records, the journal and this report's text. Each
record names who decided it and quotes the author's directing statements. The author is responsible for the content.
Large tasks ran as Claude Code workflows (a main session and subagents of the same model on disjoint files: builders,
adversarial reviewers, refuters and fixers; editors and fact-checkers). Twice, with the author's permission, AgentRA
asked OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol, through the Hermes Agent harness, a design question, sharing only the design, public
instance names and our measurements.

\paragraph{Limits.} The reviewers are instances of the agent's own model and can share its blind spots. No person or
model of another family has reviewed this report or its results. The final version will add the counts of this report's
own checks and any external review. Errors were caught by the author (three operational
failures), adversarial checks (test-rule defects before any run was read), run records (a block with kernels compiled
on the clock was voided) and fact-checks (D-030's crashed runs first reported at $P = 1$ score 0.776 and 0.782). A
subagent once sent the author's e-mail address to an outside service unasked; prompts now forbid it. Corrections and
incidents are recorded, dated, in the repository.
