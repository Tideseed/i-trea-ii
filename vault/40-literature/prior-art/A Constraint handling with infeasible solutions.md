# Prior art A — constraint handling that keeps infeasible solutions

Sweep date 2026-10-05. Question asked of each work: does it keep infeasible solutions in an evolutionary population on
purpose, and if so, by what rule, how many, and is anything protected on the feasible side?

**Rank names** follow the vault ([[D-004 Mapping I-TREA (2011) to I-TREA II]]): **Rank 0 = infeasible good**
(infeasible, key better than the incumbent; at most ⌊x·P⌋; the brief's "R2"), **Rank 1 = feasible good** (the best
⌈y·#feasible⌉ feasible, protected every generation; the brief's "R1"), **Rank 2 = rejects** (everything else; the
brief's "R3").

**Sources and rules.** Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, publisher landing pages, arXiv abstract pages, HAL,
institutional repositories and author pages. No shadow libraries, no ResearchGate/Academia.edu copies, no Wayback copies
of dead author pages as links. Paywalled-only works are listed here for completeness and are cited only in
[[Paywalled research]].

**Verification levels.** *Full text read* = the method was read in a legal full text (the version noted). *Abstract /
landing read* = publisher or repository abstract read. *Metadata only* = Crossref/OpenAlex metadata, plus whatever is
stated in the cell.

## 2. What this means for the three ranks

**Rank 0 (infeasible good).** Keeping a fixed share of infeasible solutions is established: IDEA keeps exactly
N_inf = α·N infeasible members (α = 0.2 by default), chosen by non-dominated sorting in (objectives, CVM) with crowding,
and ranks them above all feasible members (`singh2011thesis`, Alg. 3.1). I-TREA II's Rank 0 differs from IDEA in four
specific ways, and only these: (1) **membership** requires the objective to beat the incumbent (`key < inc`), where the
incumbent may come from the external MIP solver; IDEA admits any infeasible solution to its share and has no such test;
(2) **x is a cap on super-optimal infeasible survivors** — over-quota and bound-demoted infeasible-good candidates are
eliminated (`src/itrea/ea/ranks.py`), while infeasible solutions whose key does *not* beat the incumbent may still
survive in Rank 2; IDEA fills its share up to α·N and overfills it with infeasible solutions when feasible ones are
scarce; (3) the **solver's proven bound** eliminates Rank 0 points that can never be feasible at their objective;
(4) the **tournament order** is Rank 1 < Rank 0 < Rank 2 (part I), whereas IDEA's selected infeasible solutions rank
above all feasible ones. The over-quota rule (Pareto fronts in (key, V), then nearest-distance thinning) is close to
IDEA's within-set ranking; only the thinning step differs (nearest distance versus crowding).
The membership set itself — infeasible solutions whose objective beats the best feasible one — is singled out by
Farmani & Wright (2003), who use it to set penalty bounds, not to reserve slots. SMES and Mezura-Montes & Coello's MICAI
paper copy one best infeasible solution at a small rate; Lu, Deb & Singh (2018) let infeasible solutions outrank
feasible ones by fitness interpolation. FI-2Pop keeps a whole separate infeasible population that is selected by
violation alone and never evaluated on the objective, so it has neither a cap relative to the population nor an
objective test; C-TAEA does the same with an archive; PPS uses infeasible solutions in a phase, not as a standing rank.

**Rank 1 (feasible good).** Protecting a fixed share of the best feasible individuals is also established: ASCHEA's
segregational selection takes up to τ_select·μ feasible individuals first, by fitness, before any infeasible one
(`hamida2000aschea`). Takahama & Sakai's "feasible elites" are not an analogue — they are the lowest-violation points,
not the best-objective ones. I-TREA II's Rank 1 (the best ⌈y·#feasible⌉ feasible, equal-key twins moved out) is an
ordinary elitist rule; nothing in this sweep suggests it is unusual.

**Rank 2 and the comparators.** Deb's feasibility rules are the `deb` comparator and the feasibility-phase policy;
stochastic ranking is the optional `stochastic` comparator. Both are standard.

**What was not found** (a search result, not a novelty claim): a fixed population share reserved for infeasible
individuals whose objective beats the incumbent, with the incumbent possibly supplied by an external MIP solver and the
share pruned by violation. The nearest matches are IDEA (fixed α, no incumbent test), Farmani & Wright (the same set,
used for penalties), SMES / MICAI 2005 (one best infeasible copied at a small rate), BIF (fitness interpolation) and
ASCHEA (target feasible ratio, no objective-versus-incumbent test).

## 4. Citation notes and traps

- **IDEA:** cite `singh2011thesis` (Ch. 3, Alg. 3.1, α = 0.2) for the mechanism. The 2009 chapter's author order is
  Ray, Singh, Isaacs, Smith (pp. 145–165, Crossref; the thesis publication list's pp. 147–167 is wrong); the
  Singh/Isaacs/Ray/Smith order belongs to the AI 2008 paper.
- **Stochastic ranking mislink:** OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar map Runarsson & Yao (2000) to arXiv:1510.04409 (and
  OpenAlex also to hal-01215727) as "green OA". Both are Chotard, Auger & Hansen, *Markov chain analysis of cumulative
  step-size adaptation on a linear constrained problem*. Any automated open-access lookup will repeat this error.
- **ε-constrained method:** no legal open copy of any Takahama–Sakai paper was found; cite `mezuramontes2011survey`
  (§3.3) and `fan2019iepsilon`.
- **Coello's author-page files are gzipped PDFs** (`.pdf.gz`). The 2002 survey is `coello02-02.pdf.gz`;
  `coello02-03.pdf.gz` is a different paper. ResearchGate, Academia.edu and a third-party faculty mirror were seen and
  not used.
- **FI-2Pop open copy** is an author manuscript (April 2007); wording and pagination may differ from EJOR. Author order
  Kimbrough, Koehler, Lu, Wood (title page and Crossref; the repository record differs).
- **Farmani & Wright licence:** the figshare record says CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, the PDF cover 2.5; quote "author deposit in
  Loughborough University's repository".
- **Year of `rahimi2023review`:** online 2022, issue 2023; the bibliography uses 2023.
- Not covered by this sweep: the I-TREA lineage itself (topic E) and the MIP-side literature (topics B and C).
